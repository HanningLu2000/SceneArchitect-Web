SceneArchitect

Progressive 3D Scene Generation with Structural Consolidation and Semantic Grounding

Anonymous Submission
Anonymous Code
Overview of SceneArchitect. Starting from a text prompt and an initial generated view, SceneArchitect progressively expands a navigable 3D scene by alternating Structural Consolidation and Semantic Grounding, followed by depth-regularized Gaussian refinement for free-viewpoint exploration.

Abstract

With the widespread adoption of VR devices and immersive content, the demand for progressive 3D scene generation is rapidly increasing. An effective virtual scene should satisfy 1) structural consistency for free-viewpoint exploration and 2) scene-level semantic coherence with the input description. However, when a scene is expanded progressively from limited observations, existing pipelines are prone to error accumulation: small depth or alignment errors can gradually amplify into geometric distortion, while locally synthesized views may drift from the intended scene semantics, resulting in inconsistent layouts and illogical content. To address these limitations, we propose SceneArchitect, a progressive 3D scene generation framework built on two core components: Structural Consolidation, which leverages a foreground-centric reconstruction strategy together with cross-view depth alignment and fusion to preserve geometric coherence during scene growth, and Semantic Grounding, which maintains a global reference view for completion-region localization and scene-level context preservation, followed by context-aware prompt refinement to keep local synthesis controllable without drifting from the overall semantics. To further stabilize the final 3D representation, we introduce a depth-regularized Gaussian refinement strategy with staged optimization, improving robustness under imperfect monocular depth priors while retaining fine-grained appearance. Extensive experiments on diverse indoor and outdoor scenes show that SceneArchitect mitigates geometric and semantic error accumulation, achieving better structural coherence, semantic consistency, and overall visual quality than representative baselines for progressive 3D scene generation.

Approach

Method overview of SceneArchitect. SceneArchitect alternates Structural Consolidation for geometric stabilization and Semantic Grounding for scene-level coherence during progressive scene growth, followed by depth-regularized Gaussian refinement with staged optimization. The overall design explicitly targets the coupled accumulation of geometric and semantic errors in long-horizon scene expansion.

Ablation Study

We provide both qualitative and quantitative ablations to clarify the contribution of Structural Consolidation, depth-regularized Gaussian refinement, staged freezing, and Semantic Grounding under prompt perturbation. The following results illustrate not only which components matter, but also how each component changes the generated geometry and scene behavior in practice.

Ablation study of SceneArchitect. Structural Consolidation improves the geometric prior for expansion, while depth regularization and staged freezing stabilize Gaussian refinement under imperfect monocular depth priors.

Main ablation summary. Removing depth regularization leads to noisier geometry and stronger artifact accumulation, while removing staged freezing still causes unstable local deformation. This supports the claim that depth regularization alone is insufficient and that staged freezing is necessary to stabilize Gaussian refinement before appearance refinement.

Depth regularization vs. staged freezing. Without depth regularization, the rendered scenes exhibit obvious holes and incomplete geometry. Without staged freezing, local structures become stretched or fragmented, which is also visible in the depth maps. The full model yields cleaner geometry and more stable local structure.

Additional Qualitative Results

Beyond the main-paper comparisons, we further visualize SceneArchitect under broader exploration settings and more diverse scene types. These additional results highlight the practical value of the method for immersive scene browsing, controllable scene generation, and stable free-viewpoint exploration in both virtual and real environments.

360° rendered views of representative scenes. We render seven viewpoints per scene along a full 360° traversal. The three examples include a virtual seaside scene, a real indoor Christmas-cabin scene, and a real outdoor scene.
